A COUNTRY ON THE FRONT LINE 01 / CONTEXT

This is not
a future problem.
It is already here.

Across Uzbekistan, hotter summers, recurring drought and growing pressure on water systems are changing the conditions people, farms and ecosystems depend on.

Climate change and local resource pressures interact. The consequences are local — in the cities we live in, the fields that feed us and the mountains that supply our rivers.

EcoPulse brings together national assessments and peer-reviewed research. The original source links and limitations are retained below. Their statistics require a fresh editorial check before public pilot use.

01 / EXTREME HEAT THE TEMPERATURE IS RISING

UZBEKISTAN IS WARMING FASTER THAN THE GLOBAL AVERAGE

+0.27°C / DECADE

Forty degrees
is no longer rare.

Uzbekistan's Third Nationally Determined Contribution reports that the country's average air temperature has risen by around 0.27°C every ten years. It also reports that days above 40°C are increasing, especially in the Aral Sea region.

UZBEKISTAN+0.27°Caverage warming per decade
GLOBAL CONTEXTFASTERnational warming rate is reported as higher than the global average

Source: Uzbekistan Third Nationally Determined Contribution (NDC 3.0), 2025. This is a reported historical trend, not a forecast.

02 / WATER SCARCITY THE COUNTRY RUNS ON WATER

A PROJECTED SHORTAGE — NOT WATER ALREADY LOST

7 KM³ / YEAR

What happens
when the rivers
cannot keep up?

A World Bank policy report cites projections of an increase in Uzbekistan's total water shortage to as much as 7 billion cubic metres per year by 2030, and 15 billion cubic metres by 2050. The report attributes these estimates to earlier national water-resources research.

20307 km³projected annual shortage
205015 km³projected annual shortage

Source: World Bank, Uzbekistan — Policy Perspectives for Irrigation and Drainage Sector Reform (2023), citing Ministry of Water Resources (2020). These are projections, not measured annual losses.

A LANDMARK OF ENVIRONMENTAL LOSS

THE ARAL SEA.
A WARNING WE CAN SEE.

The Aral Sea disaster is not simply a symbol. It is a reminder of what happens when water systems are pushed beyond their limits. Climate change now adds further pressure to a region already shaped by decades of water mismanagement.

03 / GLACIER LOSS THE MOUNTAINS ARE OUR WATER TOWERS

A REGIONAL PROJECTION FOR THE TIEN SHAN

⅓ OF 2020 ICE MASS

One-third gone
before 2040.

A 2026 peer-reviewed study projects that glaciers across the Tien Shan mountain range will lose around one-third of their 2020 ice mass before 2040. These glaciers help sustain freshwater supplies for more than 100 million people across Central Asia.

BEFORE 2040~33%projected loss of 2020 Tien Shan ice mass
BY 210069–93%projected loss, depending on emissions scenario

Source: Van Tricht et al., Water Resources Research, published 9 February 2026. Tien Shan is a regional mountain system; these figures are not Uzbekistan-only glacier measurements.

04 / THE FUTURE SCENARIOS, NOT CERTAINTIES

2030. 2035. 2040.
What are we heading towards?

Climate projections are not exact predictions for a particular calendar year. Scientists commonly report results as multi-year periods under different emissions scenarios. Here is what the evidence lets us say responsibly.

i

Why no exact +°C figure for each year? Uzbekistan's NDC reports a historical warming rate of 0.27°C per decade. That rate should not be mechanically extrapolated as a precise 2030, 2035 or 2040 forecast. For defensible future temperature values, use the World Bank's CMIP6 multi-model projections for 20-year windows (such as 2020–2039 and 2040–2059), with the emissions scenario and baseline clearly stated.
